❗ Problem Statement

ABTalks runs a 60-day coding challenge for Indian college students, where students choose a development track and build something every day.

Although the challenge concept is valuable, the existing platform has a major user-experience problem:

The product works, but it has never been properly designed around the students who use it.

Most students access the platform primarily through their mobile phones, often late at night after college. Therefore, the platform needs to be simple, focused, motivating, and easy to use on a small screen.

Key Problems Identified
1. Lack of a Clear First Experience

A student who has never heard of ABTalks needs to quickly understand:

What ABTalks is.
What the 60-day challenge involves.
Why they should participate.
What they gain by completing it.
How their work can help build their public developer profile.

Without a clear landing experience, students may not understand the value of committing to the challenge.

2. Difficulty Tracking Daily Progress

Students need a simple way to understand:

Their current streak.
Today's task.
How many challenge days they have completed.
Their overall completion percentage.
Their achievements or standing.

Without this information being immediately visible, maintaining motivation and consistency becomes harder.

3. Challenge Tasks Need Better Guidance

A challenge day should not simply display a task title.

Students need to understand:

What they have to build.
What the expected outcome is.
What they should submit.
How to provide proof of their work.

The experience should guide the student from understanding the task → building → submitting proof.

4. Proof of Work Is Split Across Platforms

ABTalks depends on two important forms of daily proof:

GitHub repository/commit
LinkedIn post

Students need a straightforward way to submit both without confusion.

5. Real-World Student Situations

The platform must also account for realistic situations such as:

A student starting Day 1 with no streak.
A student missing a challenge day.
A student having an empty profile.
A student accessing the application from a 390px mobile screen.
Core Problem

The core problem is not the lack of a coding challenge.

The challenge already exists.

The problem is that the experience does not effectively help students understand, start, continue, track, and prove their 60-day journey.

💡 Proposed Solution

The solution is a mobile-first redesign of ABTalks that turns the 60-day coding challenge into a clear, motivating, and easy-to-follow student journey.

The redesign focuses on three essential experiences:

Discover ABTalks
       ↓
Understand the 60-Day Challenge
       ↓
Start Coding
       ↓
Track Daily Progress
       ↓
Complete Today's Task
       ↓
Submit GitHub + LinkedIn Proof
       ↓
Maintain Streak
       ↓
Earn Achievements
       ↓
Build a Public Developer Journey
1. Landing Page – /

The landing page is designed as the first interaction for students who have never heard of ABTalks.

It clearly communicates:

What ABTalks is.
The 60-day challenge concept.
Why students should participate.
The importance of daily proof of work.
GitHub + LinkedIn visibility.
Progress and achievement benefits.
A clear call-to-action to begin the challenge.

The goal is to answer the student's question:

"Why should I commit the next 60 days to this?"

2. Student Dashboard – /dashboard

The dashboard becomes the student's daily home screen.

Important information is prioritized for mobile viewing:

🔥 Current streak
📅 Today's challenge
📈 Challenge progress
🎯 Overall completion
🏆 Achievements
👤 Student standing
✅ Recent progress

Instead of making students search through the platform, the dashboard immediately answers:

"What do I need to do today?"

3. Challenge Day – /day/12

The Challenge Day screen provides the complete experience for an individual challenge day.

It clearly presents:

Day number
Challenge title
Task description
What needs to be built
Expected outcome
Helpful instructions
GitHub proof submission
LinkedIn proof submission
Submission status

The student should be able to move naturally through:

Read → Build → Submit → Complete
📱 Mobile-First Design Approach

The redesign is specifically optimized for a 390px mobile viewport, reflecting the real usage environment of college students.

The interface prioritizes:

Large touch-friendly buttons.
Clear typography.
Minimal navigation.
Strong visual hierarchy.
Short and readable content blocks.
Easy one-handed interaction.
Clear progress indicators.
Minimal unnecessary elements.

Desktop layouts are treated as a secondary responsive experience.
